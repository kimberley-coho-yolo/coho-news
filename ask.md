<section class="faq" aria-labelledby="faq-why">
  <h3 id="faq-why">The case for change</h3>

  <details id="why-now">
    <summary>Nothing bad has happened. Why do this now?</summary>
    <div class="faq-answer">
      <p class="faq-lead">No incidents isn't the same as no hazards. We don't skip pre-starts because a truck has never failed.</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>We may not know.</strong> Many breaches go unnoticed for a long time. Without monitoring, "nothing happened" can mean "nothing we saw".</li>
        <li><strong>Clients are asking.</strong> Proof of information security is becoming a standard requirement for tenders and pre-qualification in our sector.</li>
        <li><strong>It's cheapest now.</strong> We're changing platforms anyway. Building security in now costs far less than fixing things after an incident.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details id="why-change">
    <summary>Google works fine. Why change?</summary>
    <div class="faq-answer">
      <p class="faq-lead">Google has served us well. It works for email, but it doesn't give us the security, client compatibility or single home for documents we now need.</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Same tools as our clients.</strong> Many clients and suppliers work in Word, Excel and Teams. Using the same tools removes daily friction.</li>
        <li><strong>Security built in.</strong> Microsoft 365 includes the device management and protection tools that ISO 27001 needs.</li>
        <li><strong>One home for files.</strong> Every team's files get an owner, so access is controlled and nothing leaves with a leaver.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details id="why-not-wait">
    <summary>Can't we wait until things are quieter?</summary>
    <div class="faq-answer">
      <p class="faq-lead">There's never a quiet time. Waiting doesn't make it cheaper, it just makes it longer.</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Paying twice.</strong> Every month we run both platforms costs money. The plan keeps that overlap short.</li>
        <li><strong>Contract timing.</strong> Our Google contract end date sets the window. Missing it can lock us in for another term.</li>
        <li><strong>Small steps, not a big bang.</strong> A pilot, then waves, department by department. The plan avoids the Christmas shutdown, and wave timing is agreed with department heads so busy periods can be worked around.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </details>
</section>

<section class="faq" aria-labelledby="faq-people">
  <h3 id="faq-people">What it means for you</h3>

  <details id="whats-in-it">
    <summary>What's in it for me?</summary>
    <div class="faq-answer">
      <p class="faq-lead">One place for your files, the same tools our clients use, and help whenever you need it.</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Find things faster.</strong> Team files live in one place, so the latest version is easy to find.</li>
        <li><strong>No more converting.</strong> Open and send Word and Excel files without formatting problems.</li>
        <li><strong>Safer accounts.</strong> Your email and files are much harder for scammers to break into.</li>
      </ul>
      <p>The first few weeks take some getting used to, and there's a team around you while you learn.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details id="will-i-cope">
    <summary>I'm not great with technology. Will I cope?</summary>
    <div class="faq-answer">
      <p class="faq-lead">Yes, because nobody is left to work it out alone.</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Training at your site.</strong> 45-minute hands-on sessions in groups of 10 or fewer, on your own phone and computer.</li>
        <li><strong>Help beside you.</strong> Champions, daily help clinics, "do it with me" sessions and floor walkers on the day you switch over.</li>
        <li><strong>Something to keep.</strong> A printed quick reference card for everyone, plus a phone help line.</li>
      </ul>
      <p>Champions are colleagues, not IT experts. They went first in the pilot, so they remember what was confusing.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details id="lose-files">
    <summary>Will I lose my emails or files?</summary>
    <div class="faq-answer">
      <p class="faq-lead">No. Our technology partner moves your email, and a safety net catches any files that get missed.</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Email.</strong> Mail, calendar and contacts move automatically. You just sign in on the day.</li>
        <li><strong>Files.</strong> You move your own, with help. Anything left behind is copied to an archive you can reach for 90 days.</li>
        <li><strong>Records.</strong> Anything we're required to keep is exported before Google is closed.</li>
      </ul>
      <p>Google Docs and Sheets convert to Word and Excel. Most convert cleanly, but please check your important files after the move, especially complex spreadsheets. Files you've shared with clients or suppliers will need sharing again.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details id="email-address">
    <summary>Will this disrupt our work or our clients?</summary>
    <div class="faq-answer">
      <p class="faq-lead">No big bang. A small pilot, then three small waves of office staff, with email moved in the background.</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Email keeps flowing.</strong> Your existing email address doesn't change, so clients can still reach you and there's nothing to tell them.</li>
        <li><strong>Drivers aren't affected.</strong> Trucking staff keep using our app and website as normal.</li>
        <li><strong>Careful switchovers.</strong> Changes happen outside busy hours, and Google stays on until we've checked everything arrived.</li>
      </ul>
      <p>Expect a short dip while you learn the new tools. That's normal, and training and four weeks of extra support are planned for it.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details id="phone-sign-in">
    <summary>Why do I need my phone to sign in?</summary>
    <div class="faq-answer">
      <p class="faq-lead">It's like needing a key and a PIN. A stolen password on its own can't get in.</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Stops the most common attack.</strong> Most account break-ins start with a stolen or guessed password. The phone check blocks them.</li>
        <li><strong>Quick in practice.</strong> Usually just a tap to approve in the Microsoft Authenticator app.</li>
        <li><strong>Set up with help.</strong> Floor walkers set it up with you on the day. Your quick reference card shows the steps.</li>
      </ul>
      <p>The Authenticator app doesn't give the company access to your personal photos or messages. If you don't have a suitable phone, or don't want to use your own, talk to your champion or the help line about an alternative.</p>
      <p class="faq-warning"><strong>Nobody from Coho Group or our IT providers will ever ask for your password or an approval code.</strong></p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details id="drivers">
    <summary>What happens to our drivers?</summary>
    <div class="faq-answer">
      <p class="faq-lead">For most drivers, nothing. Keep using our app and website as normal.</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>No new accounts.</strong> Trucking staff don't move to Microsoft 365. Only office staff do.</li>
        <li><strong>Old accounts closed.</strong> Drivers who have Google accounts will have them closed, after we check nothing depends on them.</li>
        <li><strong>Still kept informed.</strong> Updates come through supervisors, toolbox talks, noticeboards and our app.</li>
      </ul>
      <p>Watch out for fake "your account is changing" messages. If you're not sure about one, report it.</p>
    </div>
  </details>
</section>

<section class="faq" aria-labelledby="faq-tech">
  <h3 id="faq-tech">Technology explained</h3>

  <details id="infra-software-security">
    <summary>What's the difference between infrastructure, software and cybersecurity?</summary>
    <div class="faq-answer">
      <p class="faq-lead">Infrastructure is the road, software is the truck, and cybersecurity is the locks and safety rules.</p>
      <div class="prose">
        <table>
          <thead>
            <tr><th></th><th>In trucking terms</th><th>At Coho</th></tr>
          </thead>
          <tbody>
            <tr><td><strong>Infrastructure</strong></td><td>Roads, depot, fuel, workshop</td><td>Internet, Wi-Fi, laptops, phones, accounts</td></tr>
            <tr><td><strong>Software</strong></td><td>The trucks and tools</td><td>Outlook, Excel, SharePoint, Teams, our driver app</td></tr>
            <tr><td><strong>Cybersecurity</strong></td><td>Locks, keys, pre-starts, safety training</td><td>Phone approval at sign-in, access rules, backups, ISO 27001</td></tr>
          </tbody>
        </table>
      </div>
      <p>Microsoft 365 touches all three. The security tools come built in, but they have to be switched on and set up properly. That's part of what this program does.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details id="migration-partner">
    <summary>Why do we need a migration partner?</summary>
    <div class="faq-answer">
      <p class="faq-lead">We don't have an in-house IT team. A specialist does the one-off move, while our IT contractors keep everyday systems running.</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Support isn't migration.</strong> Keeping systems running day to day is a different job from moving platforms.</li>
        <li><strong>High stakes, one-off.</strong> Moving email and setting up security are where mistakes can lose mail or lock people out.</li>
        <li><strong>Clear responsibility.</strong> The partner does the technical setup and email move. Coho leads the program, and training and communication stay with us.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details id="cost">
    <summary>What will it cost, and what do we get back?</summary>
    <div class="faq-answer">
      <p class="faq-lead">We'll know once the planning stage is done. Quoted costs go to the executive sponsor for approval before work starts.</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Where it goes.</strong> Licences for office staff, a migration partner, training and a short overlap with Google.</li>
        <li><strong>What it offsets.</strong> Closing Google accounts and unused subscriptions offsets part of the ongoing cost.</li>
        <li><strong>Savings we track.</strong> Our list of systems helps find unused and duplicate subscriptions to cancel.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </details>
</section>

<section class="faq" aria-labelledby="faq-security">
  <h3 id="faq-security">Security and how we manage technology</h3>

  <details id="iso-27001">
    <summary>Isn't ISO 27001 just paperwork?</summary>
    <div class="faq-answer">
      <p class="faq-lead">It's a certificate that helps us win work, backed by habits we mostly need anyway.</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Short policies.</strong> A handful of plain-language policies, not a library of manuals.</li>
        <li><strong>Evidence built in.</strong> Many of the controls come from Microsoft 365 settings we're switching on anyway.</li>
        <li><strong>A tender advantage.</strong> One certificate instead of answering long security questionnaires one by one.</li>
      </ul>
      <p>It does for information what our safety and quality systems already do for the rest of the business. Certification is targeted for May 2028.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details id="asset-management">
    <summary>Isn't IT asset management just more bureaucracy?</summary>
    <div class="faq-answer">
      <p class="faq-lead">It's simply knowing what technology we pay for and why, and choosing on purpose.</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>A list, not a framework.</strong> A register of our systems: who owns each one, what it costs and when it renews.</li>
        <li><strong>It saves money.</strong> Unused and duplicate subscriptions get spotted and cancelled.</li>
        <li><strong>30 minutes a quarter.</strong> One short review of big changes each quarter. No new software, no committee.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details id="new-software">
    <summary>Will I need permission to use new software?</summary>
    <div class="faq-answer">
      <p class="faq-lead">To sign the company up to something new, yes. It's a one-page check, not a committee.</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Why it matters.</strong> Every sign-up holds company data and costs money. We need to know where both are.</li>
        <li><strong>Four questions.</strong> Do we already have it? Who owns the data? How does it connect? Is it secure?</li>
        <li><strong>Ideas welcome.</strong> Got a repetitive task that could be automated? Share it with us.</li>
      </ul>
      <p>Often the check finds we already pay for a tool that does the job, which saves time and money.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details id="goes-wrong">
    <summary>What if it goes wrong?</summary>
    <div class="faq-answer">
      <p class="faq-lead">Every step has a go or no-go checkpoint, and Google stays on until we know everything arrived.</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Pilot first.</strong> Champions go first, so problems are found and fixed before anyone else moves.</li>
        <li><strong>Checkpoints.</strong> The executive sponsor signs off each stage against clear criteria, or pauses it.</li>
        <li><strong>A way back.</strong> Google isn't switched off until email and data are checked and signed off.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </details>
